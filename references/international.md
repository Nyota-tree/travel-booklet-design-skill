# International trip guidance

Use this for cross-border trips; tailor the advice to the countries and the traveler's language, documents, and confirmed bookings. Do not treat one country's practices as universal.

## Names, signs, and spoken help

- The local-script or locally used name should be prominent for finding a sign, asking for help, and matching maps. Put the traveler's-language explanation below it. Add English only when it is actually used locally or helps navigation.
- Translate high-value signs for each setting: entrance, reserved-ticket entrance, security, transfer, platform/gate, luggage storage, exit, restroom, rental, lifeguard, closure, and emergency service.
- For phrases the traveler may need to say, include the original wording, a short translation, and a pronunciation aid in the notation the user prefers (for example, English-letter approximations). Avoid claims of exact pronunciation if using approximations.

## Cross-border practicalities

- Keep local date, time zone, and station/terminal names unambiguous across flights, trains, and buses. Use the ticket's exact booked time and operator name; current schedules or platform assignments may differ.
- Separate booking requirements from general travel advice: visa or entry rules, identity document, baggage allowance, ticket validity, check-in deadline, and border/security steps should be verified against current official operator or government sources before being presented as facts.
- State where luggage is held, when it must be retrieved, and whether the traveler must return to a hotel or station before onward travel.
- For multi-city transfers, show the whole chain: current stop → pickup/metro/rail → station/terminal → service → arrival → overnight destination. Include a fallback when a missed connection, closure, or late arrival would materially change the day.
- In markets, restaurants, beaches, and shops, prioritize what the traveler will see and ask for locally: signage, ordering and payment terms, supplies/rentals, opening constraints, and exact storefront recognition. Do not assert rental availability or stock without evidence.

## Visual and document integrity

- Distinguish documentary photos from illustrations. Verify the correct branch before calling a photo a storefront or entrance.
- Include the user's original booking or ticket crop only in their private output; retain the complete scannable code and adjacent inspection fields. Add a concise translation of operational notes without replacing the source image.
- Provide offline-usable addresses and route cues where roaming or signal may be unreliable. Cite or record source links for changeable facts so the traveler can recheck them near departure.
