# Detachable destination cards

Read this only when a printed travel guide includes, or may benefit from, cards the traveler can tear or cut out. These are separate from the ordinary route cards printed on daily pages. Offer the option once for a print project; respect a "no," and do not impose cards on a digital-only guide.

## Decide what becomes a card

- Ask only for choices that affect production: whether cards are wanted, and any known paper size, duplex printing, binding/perforation, or preferred card count. If the traveler has no preference, propose a practical format rather than blocking work.
- Prioritize destinations where a pocket card helps: a hotel, booked entrance, station/terminal, complex transfer, trailhead, or place with a difficult-to-recognize entrance. The traveler may instead request one for every stop. Keep the card set in visit order and identify each card by day and stop number.
- Preserve the complete route on the normal daily pages. Tearing off a card must not remove the only copy of the next destination, critical time, or emergency instruction.

## Compose a self-contained card

- Front: prominent locally used destination name; smaller translation or purpose; full address or entrance; date/time when relevant; a compact route cue from the previous stop and **NEXT →** to the next stop. A verified photo of the exact entrance can help, but must not displace the wayfinding text.
- Back, if used: short boarding/entry steps, what to show staff, local signs or a phrase, and a fallback if the expected entrance or transport is unavailable. Use only information relevant to that destination.
- Booking numbers or ticket images belong on a detachable card only if the user wants them there and the card can keep the original code and all inspection fields readable. Do not redraw a ticket QR. Avoid putting sensitive identity data on a loose card by default; retain the full original ticket elsewhere in the private guide or source files.
- Any navigation QR must be clearly labeled as a map link, not confused with an admission or boarding code. Provide a readable address as a non-QR fallback.

## Plan for real paper, not just an on-screen dashed line

- Prefer a separate insert or card sheet when tearing a bound booklet leaf would also remove unrelated content on its reverse or facing side. If the user wants cards integrated into the booklet, plan both sides and binding position together before imposing pages.
- Keep text, QR quiet zones, and photo-identification details away from cuts, folds, binding, and perforations. Use visible cut/perforation guides only when they will not cross content. A printed dashed line does not itself create a perforation; describe the required cut or print-shop finish accurately.
- Match front/back card positions when duplex printing. Do not assume printers flip on the same edge or align perfectly; allow registration tolerance and, when needed, provide a one-sheet test before final printing.
- Choose card size and cards per sheet based on actual legibility at 100% print scale, not a fixed quota. If a card would be too small, use fewer cards per sheet or split information rather than shrinking essential text.

## Verify and hand off

- Check a rendered front and back at print size, then test-cut or fold one sheet if physical output is available. Confirm that every card stays self-contained, both sides match, the local name/address/NEXT remain readable, and any QR scans with its full quiet zone.
- Recheck the daily pages after adding cards: order, numbering, maps, and NEXT transitions must still agree. Tell the traveler whether the deliverable has true perforation, printed cut guides, or only a digital card layout.
- If the agent cannot create print-ready cut sheets, provide the card content in ordered, editable form with clear front/back grouping and cut-safe layout guidance. Do not claim the cards are physically tearable without a compatible print finish.
